Three games, one deck

The same cards, three different arguments.

Every game here is played with two standard decks and jokers, on a grid rather than in a row. What changes between them is what you are allowed to do to the board once cards are on it.

Built

Anchor

Runs and sets on a 15 by 15 board, built out from a single ace in the centre. Cards never move once played. Aces are not low cards here, they are anchors, and playing one is the only way to start a new island anywhere you like.

Tide

Every turn you may take the entire table apart and rebuild it, so long as it is all valid again by the time you are done. Melds sit as islands rather than one connected structure, and they may cross and share a card.

What is different

Rummy has no geography. These do.

In ordinary rummy a meld is a list. Here it is a line on a board, and the line has neighbours. A card can belong to a run going across and a set going down at the same time, which means the good move is rarely the obvious one.

Anchor is the tighter game: the board only ever grows, so a careless placement is permanent. Tide is the looser one: nothing is permanent, and a turn can rearrange work everyone else has done.
